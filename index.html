How the detective decides
  • Trend size is Sen's slope: the median of the slopes between every pair of years. One odd year can't drag it around.
  • Is it real? The Mann‑Kendall test asks: if there were no trend, how often would years line up this consistently? That chance is the p‑value. Below 5% we call it significant.
  • Testing many lakes at once means a few would look significant by luck. For the bloom and field variables we correct for that (Benjamini–Hochberg).
  • Seven seasons is short. “No clear trend yet” means not proven, not proven flat. Published work suggests about a decade of imagery is needed.
  • Significant isn't the same as real. A satellite record can drift. That is why each lake also shows the field record where one exists.
  • All rules were written down before the results were computed (protocol Amendments 2–4).

The long field record adds context: lakes have 6 or more well‑sampled seasons since 2000, and none shows a significant long‑term trend after the many‑lake check.

Data, methods and limits

Data

  • Cyanobacteria Index — CyAN project (EPA, NASA, NOAA, USGS), weekly lake histograms from cyan.epa.gov; ESA/EUMETSAT Sentinel‑3 OLCI; archived at NASA OB.DAAC (DOI 10.5067/S3A/OLCI/L3M/ILW/5.0).
  • NASA MERRA‑2 temperature and precipitation via the NASA POWER API.
  • NASA MODIS Terra true colour via NASA Worldview/GIBS.
  • Field chlorophyll‑a (corrected for pheophytin) — Water Quality Portal (USGS/EPA): Florida LAKEWATCH, FDEP, counties, water management districts; Florida lake records downloaded for 2000–2026, of which fall in the 115 lakes studied.
  • EPA weekly cyanoHAB forecast, archived 2025–26.

Methods

  • Bloom = lake‑median index ≥ 130 (≈ 12 µg/L chlorophyll‑a), EPA's threshold. Weeks with under half the lake visible are “unknown”.
  • Field stations are assigned to lakes by their position inside the lake outline (150 m tolerance): stations in lakes.
  • Trends: Mann‑Kendall, Sen's slope, Benjamini–Hochberg across lakes.
  • Cross‑check: per‑lake slopes 2020–2025, lake‑resampled intervals; drift model with lake and month fixed effects.
  • Forecast test: ROC‑AUC against field chlorophyll ≥ 12 µg/L, lake‑resampled intervals.

Limits

  • Nothing here is about toxins, health or whether water is safe. Check your local health department.
  • Field samples measure total chlorophyll at points; the satellite measures a cyanobacteria‑related signal over the surface.
  • Lakes share weather, so intervals are too narrow. 2025–26 field data are still arriving.
  • No dollar savings, no causes proven, nothing outside Florida's satellite‑visible lakes.
  • Not a criticism of EPA's national model: we tested one state.
